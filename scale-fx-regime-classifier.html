The book's range ∝ Tᴴ law is a ready-made regime classifier. Measure how a pair's range grows with time and it sorts itself into mean-reverting, choppy, or trending — then you trade it accordingly. Paste a price series below and let the exponent decide.

The method — two agreeing measures

Read the exponent, not the chart

The Hurst exponent (H) from R/S analysis measures how a pair's range scales with time. The variance ratio (VR) is an independent cross-check: it compares multi-day variance to what a random walk predicts. When they agree, you have a real read; when they don't, the pair is telling you it's choppy.

Mean-reverting

H < 0.5 · VR < 1

Range grows slower than random — moves reverse. Fade extremes, sell the wings, keep holds short. Overextension is your friend.

Choppy / random walk

H ≈ 0.5 · VR ≈ 1

No path memory. Directional bets have no statistical edge — shrink size, lean on carry/vol, wait for the regime to resolve.

Trending / persistent

H > 0.5 · VR > 1

Range grows faster than random — moves extend. Ride momentum & breakouts, trail stops wider, add on strength.

One honest wrinkle: R/S Hurst carries a small upward bias on short samples (a true random walk often reads H ≈ 0.55). So the tool leans on the variance ratio — cleanly centered at 1 — as the primary arbiter, with H as corroboration. That's the book's own discipline: a free-floating exponent is a lean, never a lone signal (p. 78).

Reference · how to trade each bucket

The FX regime playbook

BucketWhat it meansHow you trade itWhat kills you
Mean-revertingH<0.5 · VR<1 Deviations from a mean get pulled back. Common in rangey crosses and low-vol regimes. Fade extremes; buy support / sell resistance; sell strangles & condors; short realized vol; tight profit-takes, short holds; scale in against the move. A regime break into a trend — your fade becomes a knife-catch. Watch VR flipping above 1.
Choppy / RWH≈0.5 · VR≈1 No exploitable path memory. Most majors most of the time sit near here. Cut directional size; harvest carry where the differential pays; sell premium into the range; wait for the regime to resolve before committing risk. Overtrading noise — the coastline paradox: finer sampling just buys turnover and cost, not edge.
TrendingH>0.5 · VR>1 Moves persist and extend. Often in pairs driven by a rate-differential or policy divergence. Ride momentum & breakouts; trail stops wider; add on strength, not weakness; align carry with the trend; buy optionality/gamma into the move. Fading it (the mean-reversion reflex) and getting run over; exiting a winner too early — that's the tail that pays.

Using it well

Five rules so the exponent doesn't fool you

  1. Roll it. Compute H and VR on a rolling window (e.g. last 250 days) and watch it move. A pair migrating from choppy toward trending is often more actionable than the static label.
  2. Check two horizons. A pair can mean-revert intraday but trend on daily bars. The bucket is horizon-specific — match it to your holding period.
  3. Demand agreement. Trust the read most when H and VR point the same way. Disagreement is itself information: usually "choppy, no edge."
  4. Mind the sample. Under ~120 points the estimate is noise. And a structural break (a policy shift) resets the regime — old data lies.
  5. It sizes, it doesn't signal. The bucket tells you which kind of strategy has an edge and how to size — not the entry. Pair it with your carry, vol, and macro view.
